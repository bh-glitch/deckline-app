# Mixing Bollywood / Indian Party Music in Deckline

A practical guide for a beginner using Deckline (2 decks, auto beatmatch, EQ with kills, filter, Fade / Bass Swap / Echo Out / Filter / Cut transitions over 4-32 beats, brake, sampler, hot cues, loops).

Honesty note: web sources for Bollywood-specific DJing were thin. Phrasing, EQ and tempo rules below come from general DJ sources and apply well to Bollywood because it is 4/4 and phrase-based. The BPM-range and song-structure parts are common practitioner knowledge, not from a cited page. Treat them as rules of thumb and trust your ears.

## 1. Bollywood party music basics

**Typical tempos (rules of thumb):**
- Bollywood dance / film party songs: roughly 95-135 BPM, with many hits at 100-128.
- Badshah-style hip-hop/pop: roughly 90-135 BPM. Some tracks are really ~65-70 BPM felt at double time.
- Punjabi / bhangra pop: roughly 95-110 BPM, or 190-220 when counted at double time. Many analyzers report the half or double figure.
- Always check for half/double errors. If an analyzer says 135 but the song feels slow, it could be ~67, and the reverse also happens.

**Song structure (common pattern):**
1. Intro (often 8-16 bars, sometimes a DJ-friendly drum or dhol intro)
2. Mukhda / hook (the main catchy chorus, the part everyone sings)
3. Antara (verse) leading back to the hook
4. Rap verse (the Badshah-style 16-bar rap)
5. Instrumental or dhol break, then a final hook and an outro

**Why dhol, percussion and vocal hooks matter:** The crowd reacts to the hook and the dhol groove, not to a perfect blend. A clean, loud hook landing on the downbeat is worth more than a long, subtle mix. Your job is to protect the hooks: never let two vocals fight, and bring the next hook in on a phrase start.

**Phrasing:** Songs are built from 8, 16 or 32-bar phrases (at 4 beats a bar, that is 32, 64 or 128 beats). Start the incoming track at the start of a phrase and change sections on a phrase boundary. Deckline's 4-32 beat transition lengths map to this: 16 beats = 4 bars, 32 beats = 8 bars.

## 2. Transitions that work, mapped to Deckline

| Technique | Deckline control | Use when |
|---|---|---|
| Phrase-matched blend | Auto beatmatch + Fade, 16-32 beats | Tempos within ~6%, both tracks have a clean intro or outro. |
| Bass swap | Bass Swap, 16-32 beats | Two tracks with strong basslines at similar tempo. This is the safest "pro" blend. |
| Echo Out | Echo Out, 8-16 beats | BPMs differ a lot, or the keys clash. Old track dissolves into echo while the new one starts clean. |
| Quick cut on the hook | Cut, 4 beats (or hot cue then Cut) | You want energy: cut on the last beat of a phrase so the new hook lands on beat 1. |
| Filter sweep | Filter, 8-16 beats | Building anticipation. Sweep the outgoing track out, open the incoming one in. |
| Loop-and-mix the intro | Loop (4/8/16 beats) on the outgoing track's outro or the incoming intro | You need extra bars to line up a phrase. |
| Hook / acapella teaser | Hot cue on the next song's hook, tap it for one bar, then pull back | Teasing the crowd before the real drop. Use sparingly. |
| Meme / sound-bite drop | Load clip on a deck or sampler pad, fire in the gap | Between songs for a laugh. Keep it under ~5 s and only once or twice a set. |
| Crowd calls | Sampler: Air Horn, Siren, Rocket, Riser, Impact | Air Horn on a drop, Riser into a drop, Impact on the downbeat. |

**Sampler rules:** One hit per phrase at most, always on a beat. Never layer horn + siren + riser together. If you used a sample in the last 30 seconds, skip this one. Don't fire samples over the vocal hook; fire them in the gaps before it. Overusing air horns is the classic beginner mistake.

## 3. Big BPM jumps (e.g. 135 -> 106 -> 125)

- **Limit tempo changes to about +/-6-8%.** Beyond that, vocals sound chipmunky or slowed. A 135 -> 125 move is about 7%, so it is borderline. 135 -> 106 is far outside it.
- **Echo Out or Cut for the big jumps.** Let the old track echo away, then start the new track at its own tempo with a clean start on beat 1. Do not try to beatmatch across 25%.
- **Half / double time:** If the gap is near 2:1 (e.g. 65 vs 130), the tracks can be mixed as if they were the same tempo, with one bar of the slow track equal to two of the fast one (digitaldjtips). Check whether 106 might pair with ~53 or ~212 before bridging.
- **Build-down through a break:** Mix out of the loud section into an instrumental break or a filtered/looped section of the old track, then bring the new track in at the break. When the break ends, nudge the tempo gradually (a few BPM per phrase) if you need to bridge.
- **Meet halfway:** Digital DJ Tips suggests the "50% rule": when mixing between two tempos, blend at the midpoint so each track is only shifted by half the gap.
- Turn off master tempo / key lock only if you want the vinyl-style speed-up effect on purpose. Otherwise keep it on so pitch stays natural.

## 4. Key / harmonic mixing basics

- Camelot wheel: tracks are labelled e.g. 3A (B-flat minor). Compatible moves are the same number, +/-1 number (same letter), or the same number with the other letter (A <-> B).
- Harmonic mixing avoids melody clashes during long blends. It is not a requirement for being a good DJ (digitaldjtips).
- **When Bollywood DJs ignore it:** Short cuts, echo-outs and drops, vocal-only or percussion-heavy sections, and big tempo jumps hide key clashes. Stick to harmonic matches for long blends over 16+ beats. If the key clashes, shorten the overlap or use Echo Out or Cut.

## 5. EQ and gain rules of thumb

1. **Never two basslines at once.** Cut the incoming bass (kill the low EQ) before the blend, then bass-swap on a phrase boundary. Deckline's Bass Swap does this.
2. Match gain *before* touching EQ; beginners cut EQ first and then think the track is quiet.
3. Keep vocals from clashing: do not overlap two vocal sections. If both tracks have vocals, move the blend to an instrumental section, or cut the incoming mids/highs until the old vocal ends.
4. Keep the master meter out of the red; leave headroom for sampler hits, which are loud.
5. Use the filter for blending, not just effect: a high-pass on the outgoing track lifts the low end for the new one.

## 6. The set: three pieces in 4-6 minutes

### BPM look-ups (verify by ear)
- **Abhi Toh Party Shuru Hui Hai** (Badshah and Aastha Gill, Khoobsurat): songdata.io lists ~130 BPM, key E-flat minor (Camelot 2A). Our analyzer says ~135, which is plausible. Web estimates differ by a few BPM. Treat it as 130-135. Not confirmed by SongBPM or Tunebat, as those pages could not be reached.
- **Saturday Saturday** (Indeep Bakshi / Akriti Kakkar, Badshah rap): audiokeychain lists 136 BPM, key B-flat minor (Camelot 3A). This is a crowd-sourced database, so it is an estimate.
- **Keys:** 2A and 3A are neighbours on the Camelot wheel, so they are harmonically compatible. 130 and 136 are only ~4.6% apart, so Deckline can beatmatch them with no audible vocal distortion.
- The meme sound bite has no tempo. Ignore it for beatmatching.

### Time marks are approximate
Section times below assume ~130 BPM and ~1.85 s per bar. Songs have different structures, so use Deckline's waveform to find the real phrase starts and drop **hot cues** at them before you start (cue 1 = first beat of intro, cue 2 = first hook, cue 3 = outro start).

### The plan

**A. Start: "Abhi Toh Party Shuru Hui Hai" on Deck 1 (about 0:00 to ~2:20)**
- Press play at the first beat of the intro. Let the intro and first hook play out. This establishes the energy.
- Optional: one Riser sample into the first hook at ~0:15-0:30. That is the only sampler hit in the first half.
- Prepare Saturday Saturday on Deck 2: cue it at its first downbeat (the start of the intro), auto-beatmatch it, and pull its volume to zero.
- Around 2:05-2:15 (late in the song, before the outro), pick a phrase boundary at the end of a hook.

**B. Transition 1 - Party out, Echo Out (8 bars worth: 32 beats if you want it long, or 16 beats)**
- Use Echo Out over 16 beats starting on the last bar of a phrase. The song echoes into silence. Cut the low EQ as it fades so the echo tail is not muddy.
- You get a "silence-ish" gap of one to two beats, with only the echo tail.

**C. The meme drop (4.3 s)**
- Load the meme clip onto Deck 2's neighbour or a sampler pad. Fire it just as the echo tail ends, with nothing else playing.
- Timing: meme starts on the downbeat after the echo tail finishes, so it fills the gap by itself. Don't add an air horn on top.
- Pre-cue Saturday Saturday's first beat on Deck 2 (or its first hook) so you can launch it immediately when the meme ends.

**D. Transition 2 - Saturday Saturday slams in (Cut, 4 beats)**
- The meme ends, and on the next downbeat, press play (or trigger the hot cue) on Saturday Saturday and use Cut so it comes in with full energy.
- Option 1: start from the intro (cue 1) for a clean build.
- Option 2: jump straight to the first hook (cue 2) for a big reaction. Place an Impact on that downbeat, then nothing else for a few bars.

**E. Saturday Saturday and ending (about 2:00-2:30)**
- Let the main hook and the rap run. Play through to a natural outro or fade it with Fade over 32 beats if you have a next song.
- Keep one sample (e.g. Air Horn) in reserve for the drop after the rap, once.

**Total:** Party ~2:15-2:20, echo and meme gap ~0:08, Saturday ~2:00-2:30, so about 4:30-5:00, which is inside the 4-6 minute target.

### Summary plan
1. Start Party at the first intro beat; keep hot cues on the intro, hook and outro.
2. One Riser into its first hook; no other samples during Party.
3. Around 2:05-2:15, at the end of a hook phrase, start Echo Out (16 beats).
4. Cut the bass as it echoes away, leaving a 1-2 beat near-silent gap.
5. Fire the meme clip (4.3 s) alone on the downbeat after the echo tail.
6. When it ends, launch Saturday Saturday on the next downbeat with a 4-beat Cut, from its intro or hook cue.
7. If you start from the hook, add one Impact hit on that downbeat.
8. Beatmatch is only ~4-5% apart, so no tempo workaround is needed; 2A to 3A is a harmonic neighbour.
9. Keep Saturday going for ~2 minutes, with at most one Air Horn on a drop.
